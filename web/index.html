Problem Statement & Paradigm Shift

The Laboratory Assay Latency Bottleneck

Why do flotation circuits suffer from sudden impurity surges and wasted chemicals? The root cause is a fundamental disconnect between high-frequency sensor streams and slow laboratory quality assays.

Traditional Control Loop

60 – 120 Min Delay
  • 1 Raw ore feed grade fluctuates continuously (e.g. silica surges from 12% to 22%).
  • 2 Operators run fixed heuristic dosing tables while slurry travels through 7 columns.
  • 3 Grab samples are physically carried to the laboratory for XRF / titration assays.
  • 4 Result: 2 hours of off-spec concentrate (> 2.0% SiO₂) produced before operators even realize dosing was insufficient, incurring huge penalty fines.

Mintek AI Closed-Loop Solution

< 0.005 Seconds (Real-Time)
  • 1 Telemetry streams continuously into the AI Soft Sensor every 20 seconds.
  • 2 AI model forecasts silica concentrate impurity (%) and iron recovery instantaneous curves.
  • 3 Prescriptive optimizer calculates minimal Amina and Starch flow setpoints to ensure SiO₂ ≤ 2.0%.
  • 4 Result: Real-time proactive control, zero penalty shipments, and 8%–12% reduction in toxic chemical overdosing.

1. Quartz Silica Collection (Amina)

SiO₂ + R-O-(CH₂)₃-NH₃⁺ Hydrophobic Quartz

Cationic ether amine molecules adsorb electrostatically onto negatively charged silica surfaces, orienting their hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails outward to cling onto ascending air bubbles.

2. Iron Oxide Depression (Starch)

Fe₂O₃ + Causticized Starch Hydrophilic Shield

Corn starch macromolecules rich in amylose selectively complex with surface iron atoms, forming a dense hydrophilic steric barrier that prevents air bubbles from attaching to valuable iron particles.

3. Slurry Alkalinity Window

Optimal Window: pH 10.0 – 10.5 (Zeta Potential)

Slurry pH controls particle zeta potentials. Below pH 9.5, quartz surfaces lose negative charge. Above pH 11.0, amines deprotonate into neutral molecules, causing catastrophic recovery drops.

1. Reverse Cationic Flotation Kinetics

In reverse cationic flotation of iron ore, the rate of quartz removal in an aerated column cell follows first-order reaction kinetics:

dC_silica / dt = -k · C_silica · S_b

Where k is the flotation rate constant (driven by collector surface coverage and pulp pH), and S_b is the bubble surface area flux (S_b = 6 · J_g / d_b). Our machine learning soft sensor models this complex interaction without requiring prohibitive physical bubble size measurements.

2. Metallurgical Mass Balance & Recovery Formulation

The two-product metallurgical recovery rate ($R$) calculates the percentage of valuable metal (iron) successfully retained in the concentrate stream versus the initial feed:

R = [c · (f - t)] / [f · (c - t)] × 100%

Where f is feed iron grade, c is concentrate iron grade, and t is tailings iron grade. By maintaining predicted silica under 2.0%, the concentrate iron grade is naturally stabilized above 65.0% Fe.

3. Data Hygiene & Industrial Resampling

Key Student Finding: The raw Kaggle Samarco dataset contains 737,453 records sampled at 20-second intervals. Due to hydraulic residence time (slurry takes 20–45 minutes to transit 7 columns), adjacent 20-second records are highly collinear. Resampling to 1-minute intervals and engineering 30-minute rolling residence-time windows dramatically improves model generalization.
